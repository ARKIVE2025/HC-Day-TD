💡 HC Day Trader Sim — Companion Guide

What is this?

HC Day Trader Sim v3.0 is a practice day-trading and strategy-building simulator. Trade against randomly generated data or a live real-market feed, with $25,000 in simulated cash — zero real money at risk either way.

The tabs

  • Trade — place practice orders against a simulated chart or a live data feed, bar by bar or in real time.
  • Backtest — build a rule-based strategy and see how it would have performed historically.
  • AI Advisor — ask questions about charts, indicators, and risk management.
  • Learn — short lessons and a glossary for beginners.
  • Settings — change the market regime, order execution assumptions, or reset your account.

Important

In Simulated mode, every price is randomly generated. In Live mode, prices are real (via your own Finnhub connection) but every order you place is still simulated — nothing here is a real trade or financial advice.

Choosing your data feed

Use the Simulated / Live toggle above the chart. Simulated mode gives you Step/Play controls to move through generated data at your own pace. Live mode streams a real symbol in real time via Finnhub — once connected, price updates arrive on their own.

Step by step

  1. Choose your order type: Market (fills immediately), Limit (fills at your price or better), or Stop (triggers once price reaches your level).
  2. Choose Buy Long (betting price rises) or Sell Short (betting price falls).
  3. Set your Position Size in shares, dollars, or % of your cash.
  4. Optionally set a Stop Loss and Take Profit price — the position closes itself when price reaches either one.
  5. Submit the order. Market orders fill right away (with realistic slippage and commission applied); limit/stop orders wait under Pending Orders until triggered.
  6. Manage open positions any time from Open Positions, individually or with "Close All."

Every closed trade appears in Portfolio & History with its realized profit or loss, net of simulated commissions and slippage.

Candlesticks

Each candle represents the Open, High, Low, and Close (OHLC) for one time period. A green (bullish) candle closed higher than it opened; a red (bearish) candle closed lower. The thin lines above and below the body are wicks — they mark the high and low for that period.

Indicators (toggles above the chart)

  • SMA (Simple Moving Average) — the average closing price over a set lookback period (20 bars here). Smooths noise to reveal trend direction.
  • EMA (Exponential Moving Average) — like SMA but weights recent price more heavily, so it reacts faster to new moves.
  • BB (Bollinger Bands) — a volatility envelope plotted around a moving average. Price tends to revert toward the middle band and stretches the bands during high volatility.
  • RSI (Relative Strength Index) — a 0-100 momentum oscillator. Readings above 70 suggest overbought conditions; below 30 suggest oversold.
  • MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence) — the spread between a fast and slow EMA, plus a signal line. Crossovers and histogram direction flag momentum shifts.
  • Vol (Volume) — shares traded per bar. Rising volume on a breakout adds conviction; low volume moves are less reliable.

Market Structure

Toggle Structure to auto-mark swing highs and swing lows. Consecutive higher highs and higher lows (HH / HL) define an uptrend; lower highs and lower lows (LH / LL) define a downtrend. A break in that pattern is often the first sign of a trend change.

Trend Lines

Click Trend Line, then click two points on the chart to draw a support or resistance line connecting them — the same way you would on a real charting platform. Use Clear to remove all drawn lines.

Market Sessions

When running in Live mode, a session bar shows the current US equity market session in Eastern Time: Pre-Market (4:00-9:30 AM ET), Regular Session (9:30 AM-4:00 PM ET, the most liquid period), After-Hours (4:00-8:00 PM ET), or Closed. Liquidity and volatility both change sharply outside the regular session — worth watching before you size a position.

Hover over any toggle or field in the app itself for a quick one-line explanation.

What backtesting does

Instead of trading bar-by-bar yourself, you define rules (e.g. "RSI is below 30") and the tool runs that strategy automatically across 500 bars of fresh simulated data.

How to use it

  1. Add one or more entry conditions — all of them must be true to trigger a trade.
  2. Set position size %, stop loss %, and take profit %.
  3. Click Run Backtest to see win rate, total P&L, profit factor, and max drawdown.

This is a fast way to see whether an idea has any merit before "trading" it live on the Trade tab.

What it's for

The AI Advisor answers questions about charts, indicators, and risk management in plain language. It reads your current price and indicator values to give context-aware answers.

Setup required

You must connect it yourself under the AI Advisor's Connection tab, using one of: LM Studio (free, runs on your computer), Groq, or DeepSeek. Nothing is pre-connected.

A caution

AI answers can be wrong or oversimplified. Treat it as a study aid, not a source of guaranteed-correct facts or trading signals.

Is this using real stock prices?

No. Every chart is randomly generated on the spot. Nothing here reflects any real company or market.

Can I lose real money?

No. The $25,000 balance is simulated play money.

Where is my data saved?

Your AI connection settings are saved in this browser only. Trade history resets if you reset the account or reload with a new simulation.

Why didn't my strategy trigger any trades in Backtest?

Your entry conditions may be too strict, or the random data for that run simply never met them — try loosening a condition or re-running.
